Three pieces, one job

The book explains the decisions. Gap keeps the financial record. Streak keeps the logging alive.

The bookThe Anti-Paycheck TrapWhy the gap closes on people who work hard, and what to do about it in order. You read it once, then keep it for reference.
The trackerBeFree GapRecords income, living costs and transfers, shows what is safe to spend until payday, and plans the paychecks ahead.
The habitBeFree StreakSupports small daily actions and coming back after a missed day. A habit tick does not certify complete financial records.

The book sets the target. Gap shows the real number and what is eating it. Streak makes the daily entry a thing you do without deciding.

Gap and Streak are two separate apps, each with its own icon on your phone or computer. They do not share data or pass anything between them: Gap holds the money record, Streak holds your habits. Another device, browser or profile keeps its own separate copy of each.

One responsibility

Gap’s numbers and suggestions are only as good as what you enter. Check them against your statements before you act. Your records stay on this device unless you export them. Nothing is sent to us, and there is no account to lose. Save a dated backup from each app every week and after setup or major changes. Encrypted backup in Settings locks the file with a passphrase that nobody can recover for you.

Before any screen

The gap is a number,
not a character flaw

The gap is received income less living costs and required debt payments. Savings and extra debt payments assign that gap.

Most people never learn their number. They know roughly what they earn and roughly what rent costs, and the space between those two facts stays a feeling instead of a figure. A feeling cannot be widened. A figure can.

The eight phases

One spine, and it is the book’s. Start at the lowest phase you cannot yet tick, not at phase one because the list starts there. The full checklist for each is Appendix A of The Anti-Paycheck Trap.

1 · Stabilize Clears when one full pay period passes with no overdraft and no late fee.
2 · Map Clears when your gap is positive for a full month and one transfer has cleared.
3 · Protect Clears when Buffer Rung 1 is funded and nothing has gone on a card for 30 days.
4 · Build Clears when it runs without you thinking about it.
5 · Scale Clears when one engine has produced income in three separate months.
6 · Invest Clears when one automatic investment has cleared and the fund is chosen.
7 · Walk Clears when savings cover one full year of your costs.
8 · Cross Clears when the portfolio earns more in a year than you put into it.
If your gap is negative right now

Nothing has gone wrong. You have just finished the hardest part, which is looking. The book has a short section written for exactly this, called When the Math Doesn’t Close, and it comes before everything else.

Where they live

Both apps install from one page, on the phone or computer you will actually use:

The installation sheet in this bundle shows the steps for iPhone, Android, Windows and Mac.

The one rule that protects everything

Each app keeps its record on this device, inside the installed app. Another browser, profile or device has its own separate storage, and the apps do not sync. Keep dated backups. Require a PIN to open hides the screen; it does not encrypt the record.

When data can be lost

Clearing site data, private browsing, device loss or storage cleanup can remove records. Check the installed app and your backups before clearing anything.

Back up every week

In each app: Settings, Save a backup file. Keep private, dated copies. Restore a Gap file in Gap and a Streak file in Streak. Save the current record first: restoring replaces it.

Open each app once while online, then test opening it with no signal. Updates arrive over the network the next time you are online.

Money and transfers

Get this one distinction right and every screen in Gap reads itself. Get it wrong and the app will quietly give you the wrong advice for months.

IncomeFixedVariable
What it isMoney arrivingRecurring billsOther spending
ExamplesPaycheck, tips, freelance receiptsRent, phone, insurance, the car payment, card minimumsGroceries, gas, takeout, that thing on the way home
BehaviorAnything that lands in your handsRecurring; the amount may changeCan be essential or optional

Gap = received income − living costs − required debt payments. A transfer moves or assigns money: savings, tax reserves, funds and extra debt. Paying off a card does not count a recorded purchase twice.

Fixed means recurring

A utility bill may change amount and still belong under Fixed. Groceries are usually Variable. Essential versus optional is a separate decision.

A second axis, and it is not the same one

Gap also asks whether a category is essential or optional. Groceries vary but are essential; a streaming plan is fixed but optional. The until-payday figure and the suggestions run on that second answer.

These transfers assign the gap; they do not reduce income or create another expense.

The screen you will see most

Reading your gap

Everything else in Gap is a way of explaining this one number.

Open Insights. Where you stand sits at the top. The green bar is everything that came in. The bar below it is everything that went out: gold for fixed bills, orange for variable spending. What is left over, in olive, is your surplus, and that is your gap.

Both bars are drawn to the same scale, so the picture is honest. When money out passes money in, the shortfall shows hatched and the number turns red. Beneath the number sits the change against last month.

The chips underneath change what every card on the screen is counting: this month, the last 30 days, the last 90, the last 12 months, all time. Read the gap in This month during the month, because that is the number you can still change. Read it in Last 90 days on a Sunday, because that is the one that says whether anything is improving.

Where typical comes from

Typical uses the months you have closed. Historical charts still show records from open months and may be incomplete. No entries does not mean zero spending.

This is a refund. Money back on something you bought. It lowers spending in that category instead of counting as income, which keeps your income line honest.

Planned, not done. An entry you have not confirmed yet waits under Coming up. Gap treats it as still owed until you say it happened.

The split asks where the protected available amount should go. Confirm a current checking balance and the obligations before payday; do not automatically divide the whole deposit.

Where is this money going? Not next week, when it has already gone somewhere. Now, in the eleven seconds after it arrived.

When you confirm a paycheck and you have a goal or a debt, Gap offers this split. Drag the two marks; the bar and the three rows update as you move them. If Gap is missing today’s balance or your bills, it asks for those first.

Planned until you say otherwise

Leave I’ve already moved this money unticked and the shares wait under Coming up until you confirm them. Tick it once the transfers are actually done. The app records what happened; your bank decides what happens.

Your split is remembered

The percentages are editable examples, not requirements.

Leave it switched on

The split is optional. Keep transfers planned until money moves, then confirm the existing entry. Do not add a second completed copy of the same transfer.

Avalanche or snowball

Money, Debts asks you once to pick Highest rate first (costs least) or Smallest first (closes an account sooner), and every screen follows it, including this one. The debt list is ordered the same way. The worst choice is spreading spare money evenly across all of them.

The order that works

Protect necessities and urgent payments. Build an affordable starter cushion. Review employer match, vesting and affordability alongside costly debt. Then expand reserves and long-term investing as circumstances allow.

Plan, then confirm

Bills that enter
themselves

A repeat is a planned bill or paycheck. It appears on the expected date, but it does not enter completed totals until you confirm the amount and that it happened.

Without repeats your gap looks enormous on the 2nd and terrible on the 15th, because rent has not been typed in yet, and neither figure has ever been true.

Setup builds these from the bills you filled in. Anything you missed is added in Money, Ledger, under Paychecks and bills that repeat. Each line shows the day, the amount and a switch. Tap a line to edit it.

On the Today screen each one waits under Coming up with Paid and Skip.

If an amount moves a little each month, put in the typical figure and correct the entry when the real bill arrives. A repeat that is twenty dollars off beats a bill that is missing entirely. Switching a repeat off stops future entries without deleting the history.

18
Careful here

A repeat records a bill. It does not pay one. Nothing in either app moves money. Set the autopay at your bank, then confirm it here.

Sinking funds, for the bills that are not monthly

For a predictable bill that is not monthly, create a sinking fund in Money, Goals with its target, current balance and due date. Gap works out the set-aside per paycheck. Confirm real transfers with Set aside and record withdrawals with Pay from it; a plan does not prove the money is already there.

A fund knows its own due date, so it can tell you when it is behind.

What to do next

Your next best step organizes prompts from the figures you enter.

It sits on the Today screen: one suggestion at a time, with the arithmetic behind it and a button that acts on it.

The order is deliberate: what is urgent first, then what the move is worth over a year, then how hard it is. Ten pieces of advice at once is the same as none, so the card shows one and keeps the rest behind more.

Prompts can flag debt interest, changing spending, estimated tax-reserve shortfalls, funds and buffer coverage. A reserve estimate is not a tax bill.

Today’s money check: the two-minute version, for a day when you are not reading anything.

Turn advice into a to-do

A call you meant to make lives better on a list with a due date than in your head. When a suggestion means a one-off job, such as calling about a rate, add it to Money to-dos in Streak. It takes ten seconds and the two apps stay separate.

Most money trouble is a timing problem. Plan lays out the next few paychecks and which bills each one has to cover.

Paychecks ahead. Each paycheck, the scheduled bills due before the next one, and what is left. A short line here is a warning weeks in advance, not on the morning rent is due.

Reference

If your pay changes

Standard budgeting assumes a number that arrives on the same day for the same amount. If your income moves week to week, that advice does not fail you slowly. It fails you immediately.

In Gap, open Settings, Pay schedule, and tick My paycheck amount changes. Every forecast is then built from your lowest recent paycheck rather than the average, and an Income that varies panel appears on Insights. If you told setup your pay moves, it is already on.

Your baseline week Annual essential costs divided by 52. Compare it with a lean month of actual receipts. If receipts are below essentials, seek income or support rather than calling the shortfall a spending allowance.
This week runs on last week Spend the money you earned two weeks ago, not the money you are earning now. Getting one week ahead is the hardest part, and it changes everything after.
Estimated taxesForm 1040-ESFor freelance, gig or other income with no tax withheld. Turn on the tax reserve to see each quarterly due date, moved to the next business day when it falls on a weekend or holiday, and what to set aside.
Mileage logFor gig driving, deliveries or other work trips: date, miles, destination and business purpose, the details the IRS asks for, with a spreadsheet export.
The trap this exists to break

A good week feels like a raise, so it gets spent like one. A bad week feels like a crisis, so it gets covered with a card. Over a year the weeks roughly cancel out, but the spending and the interest do not.

BeFree Academy does not provide tax advice, and an accountant will give you a better number than any app can.

The quiet one

The habit that keeps it
alive

A workable routine helps you return to the record.

BeFree Gap is only as good as the entries in it, and the entries only exist if you keep making them on days when nothing feels urgent. That is a habit problem, not a money problem, and it needs its own tool.

Streak puts did I do the thing on a screen every day and makes the unbroken run visible enough to be worth protecting. It opens with a library of money habits rather than a blank page: Log today’s spending sits at the top, because every chart in Gap is downstream of whether you logged yesterday. Start that one.

Name a habit as an action

Log today’s spending works because you either did or you did not. Be better with money cannot be ticked, which means it cannot be a habit.

The date, the run, and one row per habit due today.

A small version supports continuity: I did the small version keeps the day without pretending it was a full one. The small version keeps your run. It does not complete the day’s record.

A day you mark as no-spend ticks Log today’s spending by itself. Streak does not read Gap, so everything else you tick yourself.

The three rules that govern a run
All of it, or none of itThe all-habits streak counts a day when every due habit is done. The 30-day rate also shows partial progress.
A rest day is invisibleA day with nothing scheduled is skipped entirely. A habit set to weekdays keeps its run alive across every weekend.
Today stays openAn unfinished today never breaks the run. The day is judged only once it has passed.
One habit. Not five.

Five habits means five chances to break the day, and a broken day breaks the run. Start with one, add a second only after a month of clean days, and never run more than four. A low completion rate is rarely a discipline problem. It is a design problem, and the fix is fewer habits.

When you break it

You will lose a run. Check the month grid in Progress first, because sometimes you did the thing and forgot to tick it, and backfilling is honest. Then look at Coming back, and start again today rather than on Monday. A run of 1 beats a plan to begin later.

Challenges, when you want a push

The Challenges tab offers short, dated runs: a 30-day money reset any time, and seasonal ones such as a New Year reset, tax season, back-to-school and No-Spend November. Joining one adds its habits for that window only. Share on the Today card makes an image of your run, with no money figures in it.

The other list

One-off jobs, and where
they live

Not everything is a habit. Some money jobs are done once and then gone.

Cancel a subscription. Call about a rate. Set up an autopay. Ask for the self-pay discount on a bill. These have no rhythm, so putting them among your habits would only break a run you were keeping honestly.

Streak keeps them apart, under Money to-dos. A to-do can have no date, a date, or Before payday. And it has a field most to-do lists never think to ask for: what it is worth per year.

One-time jobs, with ready-made ideas to start from.

Why the yearly figure matters

A $16 monthly subscription suggests $192 a year if the cancellation lasts. Done to-dos add up their yearly figures. A completed task is not proof of that saving: confirm the actual cost change in Gap and review the result later.

30
The ten-minute Sunday, in order
  1. Set the range to Last 90 daysIn Insights. You are looking for the shape of your life, not the last four days of it. One month is weather.
  2. Read the gap and the directionWider or narrower than last month? That single comparison is the score.
  3. Read Pace checkIf this month is running above the typical line you have days to correct it. On the 30th you would have none.
  4. Read the suggestion, then choose exactly oneCancel one subscription. Move $50 to the target debt. Hold one category for a week. One.
  5. Do it now, not laterThe suggestion has a button on it for a reason. Nothing that gets deferred to Wednesday happens on Wednesday.
  6. Answer the three questions in StreakDo this week’s review: what worked, what got in the way, one adjustment. Two minutes is plenty.
  7. Read the 12-week barsRising, flat or sliding. If it is sliding, cut a habit rather than promising yourself more effort.
What the whole thing costs

Ten minutes a week is about 8.7 hours a year.

Six ways to make
the app lie to you

Gap is only ever as accurate as what you feed it. Every one of these produces a number that looks perfectly plausible and is completely wrong.

Logging transfers as income

Moving $200 from savings to checking is not income. Log it as a transfer, and only count money entering your household from outside it.

Skipping the small ones

A $4 coffee feels beneath recording. Forty of them is $160, which is most of a utility bill.

Confusing fixed with constant

A recurring utility bill can be Fixed even when its amount changes. Update the planned amount, confirm the real payment and mark whether it is essential.

Logging the day it cleared

Use the date the money was spent. Otherwise your charts describe your bank’s processing schedule.

Judging a category from one month

One dentist visit does not mean you have a healthcare problem. Switch to Last 90 days before you conclude anything.

Never setting up repeats

The most common of all, and the single biggest source of a misleading gap. The app raises it as a suggestion on its own.

The rest of the app

Six things worth
knowing

Everything in the first pages is the daily loop. These six are the parts you will meet later, once the record has something in it.

Guides on a category

In Insights, Where it goes prints a monthly guide beside groceries, dining out, gas, shopping and entertainment, and flags one running hot before the month ends. Guides are common starting points, not verdicts.

Owed to you

Money you lent is not spending and it is not a debt. It sits at the foot of Money, Debts, outside the gap, so it stops making your month look worse and you stop forgetting it exists.

Saved over time

The chart at the top of Money, Goals shows net transfers and withdrawals for goals and funds. Money held for future bills is not an investment. Goal dates use each goal’s own transfers in closed months.

Biggest movers

In Insights, the categories that changed most against the month before, largest first. Read it on a Sunday: it names the thing to look at without you hunting through twenty lines.

Export and print

Settings holds a backup file that restores everything, an encrypted version of it, a spreadsheet (CSV) for an accountant, and Print this month. The backup is the one that matters. Use it.

The colors in the bar

In Where you stand, money out is split where the fixed bills end and the variable spending begins. One you change with a phone call. The other you change a hundred small times.
